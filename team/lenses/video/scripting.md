# Video scripting

Write for one viewer, one idea, one action. The hook is written first, the payoff second, the middle last.

## Hook (0 to 3 s)
Open on the viewer's problem, a bold claim you can prove, a visual pattern break, a result shown before the method, or a specific number. Never open on a logo, a greeting or "in this video". The first frame must read as a still image. Write 5 hook variants per script; ship the strongest, keep two for testing.

## Structures
- **Problem → Agitate → Solve** (ads, SaaS pain-led): pain in the viewer's words, cost of the pain, product as the shortest path, proof, CTA.
- **Before → After → Bridge**: show the after first when the result is visual.
- **Demo-led** (SaaS): outcome in one sentence, then the 2 or 3 clicks that get there, real screen, real data, no menus tour.
- **Feature launch**: what changed, who cares, 10-second demo, where to find it.
- **Founder / talking head**: one belief, one story that earned it, one takeaway.
- **Testimonial / UGC-style**: skeptical start, specific moment it clicked, concrete result with a number, unpolished delivery on purpose.
- **Tutorial**: promise the result, number the steps, show every step, recap in 5 seconds.
- **Listicle / "3 mistakes"**: count down, strongest item last, each item ≤ 8 s in short-form.
- **Long-form YouTube**: cold open with the payoff teased, chaptered arc, a re-hook every 60 to 90 s, end screen leading to the next video.

## Length by job
Paid ad 15 to 30 s (6 s bumper variant). Reel/Short/TikTok 15 to 45 s; go longer only when the idea needs it. SaaS demo 60 to 90 s. Explainer 90 to 120 s. Long-form 8 to 15 min when retention supports it.

```text

| 0-2   | hook        | ...                         | ...            | ...                     | SFX/beat  | stop      |
| 2-8   | problem     | ...                         | ...            | ...                     |           | relate    |

| 20-26 | proof       | ...                         | ...            | ...                     |           | believe   |
| 26-30 | CTA         | ...                         | ...            | end card                |           | act       |
```

## Writing rules
- Spoken words: short sentences, contractions, no adjectives that do not change meaning. Read it aloud; cut what you stumble on.
- One number beats three adjectives. Specific beats clever.
- On-screen text is not a transcript: 3 to 6 words per card, one card per beat.
- The CTA is one verb and one destination. Match funnel stage: cold traffic learns, warm traffic tries, hot traffic buys.
- Localize, do not translate: idioms, numbers, currency and humor per market.
- For SaaS: show the real product, real screen, real data. Fake dashboards and lorem ipsum destroy trust.
- Every claim gets a source or a proof shot. Unapproved claims are marked in the script, not silently softened.

## Retention design
Change something visible every 2 to 4 s in short-form (shot, text, zoom, prop). Place a re-hook where viewers drop: after the problem, before the proof. Never resolve the tension before the payoff. End on the CTA, not on a fade.
