# Critique & Finish Gate

Critique should be anchored to product goals, not personal adjectives.

## Two-pass self-review

### Pass A — product/UX
Review before aesthetic nitpicks:
- first-read object;
- primary job/action;
- information needed at decision time;
- architecture;
- discoverability;
- states;
- errors;
- recovery;
- permissions;
- efficiency;
- responsive preservation.

### Pass B — visual/craft
Then:
- hierarchy;
- composition;
- specificity;
- typography;
- spacing;
- alignment;
- color;
- iconography;
- density;
- component consistency;
- accessibility;
- optical details.

Do not let visual polish hide structural problems.

## Genericness test

Ask:
"Could this exact screen, after changing logo and labels, belong to five unrelated SaaS products?"

If yes, identify **why**:
- interchangeable card grid;
- no domain-object hierarchy;
- generic metrics;
- template navigation;
- decorative brand only;
- no workflow-specific interaction.

Replace with product-specific structure, not merely stronger styling.

## Cognitive load

Check:
- number of competing primary actions;
- visible choices;
- mode/context changes;
- jargon;
- recall requirements;
- nested panels;
- repeated decisions.

High density is not automatically high cognitive load. Well-aligned data can be dense and cognitively efficient.

## Quality gate

Implementation-ready work should satisfy:

### Product
- user/job clear;
- critical flows represented;
- domain vocabulary coherent.

### UX
- navigation;
- states;
- errors/recovery;
- permissions;
- empty/loading;
- destructive safety.

### Visual
- deliberate hierarchy;
- coherent rhythm;
- product-specific visual language;
- no unexplained decorative defaults.

### Brand
- approved direction consistently expressed;
- not over-applied to task surfaces.

### System
- repeated decisions encoded;
- components/tokens coherent;
- no uncontrolled one-offs.

### Accessibility
- contrast/focus/touch/keyboard implications understood;
- information not color-only;
- motion restrained.

### Responsive
- job preserved at target widths.

## Critique language

Weak:
"Feels dated."

Strong:
"Four equal-weight summary cards compete with the overdue-payment queue, even though the queue drives the user's daily action."

Weak:
"Make it premium."

Strong:
"Reduce nested borders, tighten table density and strengthen type hierarchy so the interface feels precise rather than padded/decorated."

## Finish decision

Ashley may say:
- **ready to iterate with users**;
- **ready for implementation**;
- **not ready**, with concrete blockers.

Do not declare perfection.
