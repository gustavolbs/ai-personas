# Brand Identity

Brand identity begins with meaning, not a palette.

## Brand brief

Resolve:
- what the product does;
- audience;
- category;
- positioning;
- promise;
- differentiation;
- desired associations;
- undesired associations;
- personality;
- trust requirements;
- cultural/geographic context;
- places the identity must work.

## Semantic territories

Before visual concepts, define 3–5 semantic territories.

Example for an operations SaaS:
- control / orchestration;
- clarity / visibility;
- partnership / coordination;
- resilience / continuity.

Each territory should produce different visual opportunities.

## Visual territories

For each promising territory define:
- typography character;
- color strategy;
- geometry;
- logo opportunity;
- iconography;
- imagery;
- motion;
- layout grammar;
- voice;
- risks/clichés.

The territory must remain recognizable beyond one hero screen.

## Category conventions

Identify conventions:
- what builds trust;
- what users expect;
- which conventions are overloaded;
- where differentiation is safe.

Do not be different at the expense of comprehension.

## Identity system

An identity can include:
- logo system;
- type system;
- color;
- shape language;
- iconography;
- imagery;
- illustration;
- data visualization;
- motion;
- voice;
- spatial/layout patterns.

A brand is not "logo + hex codes."

## Brand distribution

For operational products, brand often works best through:
- typography;
- color semantics;
- precise component character;
- navigation;
- icons;
- selected moments of delight.

Do not force loud brand expression into every data row.

## Voice

Define dimensions rather than adjectives only.

Example:
- direct > playful;
- precise > poetic;
- calm > urgent;
- human > bureaucratic;
- confident > hype.

Create examples:
- CTA;
- empty state;
- error;
- success;
- destructive confirmation.

## Validation

Test identity:
- light/dark contexts;
- app icon/favicon;
- dense product UI;
- marketing;
- small sizes;
- print/export if relevant;
- monochrome;
- accessibility.

Record rejected territories and why so Ashley does not rediscover them later.
