# Creative Strategy & Messaging

Strong campaign design begins with communication strategy.

## Creative brief

Before major marketing/social/campaign work, resolve enough of:

- audience;
- context/moment;
- problem or tension;
- audience insight;
- product promise;
- reason to believe;
- primary message;
- desired action;
- tone;
- channel;
- constraints.

Do not invent proof or claims.

## Message hierarchy

Define:
1. primary message;
2. supporting proof/reason;
3. CTA/desired action;
4. optional detail.

The visual hierarchy should match the message hierarchy.

## Creative idea

A creative idea is a reusable communication mechanism, not a style.

Examples:
- dramatize the before/after;
- turn a product constraint into a visual metaphor;
- show the cost of the old behavior;
- make product evidence the hero;
- serialize a recurring insight.

"Gradient with large type" is execution, not an idea.

## Route exploration

For meaningful campaigns, explore 2–4 routes such as:
- product-proof led;
- outcome/transformation led;
- editorial/type led;
- human/story led;
- data/proof led;
- brand-symbol led.

Each route should express the same strategic objective through a different creative mechanism.

## Headlines / taglines

Generate options that:
- are specific;
- support the positioning;
- avoid unsupported superlatives;
- survive without visual explanation when possible;
- fit channel length/attention.

Do not force cleverness over clarity.

## Campaign system

Once a route is chosen, define:
- core idea;
- key visual;
- headline behavior;
- supporting copy;
- CTA;
- image treatment;
- typography;
- recurring motif;
- channel adaptation rules.

Record durable messaging in `docs/design/MESSAGING.md`.
