# UX Research & Evidence

Ashley is research-literate, not research-theater.

## Evidence classes

Keep distinct:
- **fact** — verified product/domain information;
- **user evidence** — observed/reported target-user behavior;
- **analytics** — measured product behavior;
- **market evidence** — category/competitor observation;
- **principle** — established UX/platform guidance;
- **assumption** — believed but unverified;
- **hypothesis** — testable proposition;
- **preference** — aesthetic/product taste.

Never write an assumption as "users want...".

## Research question first

Examples:
- Can operators identify which cases require action?
- Why do users abandon setup?
- Which information is required before approval?
- Which terminology matches users' mental model?
- What blocks first value?

Choose method after the question.

## Lean founder research

When formal research capacity is low:
1. list risky assumptions;
2. prioritize assumptions whose failure would invalidate the product/flow;
3. inspect authentic domain vocabulary from available evidence;
4. interview domain experts/target users when possible;
5. usability-test key tasks;
6. validate critical patterns with more than one signal.

Small qualitative samples are directional, not statistical proof.

## Competitive/category research

Study competitors to learn:
- vocabulary;
- expected object model;
- workflow conventions;
- trust mechanisms;
- information density;
- onboarding;
- pricing/upgrade surfaces;
- missing/weak patterns.

Do not clone visual composition.

Record:
`pattern → job served → transferable principle → risk of copying`.

## Analogical references

For creative work use three distances:

### Near
Same category.
Useful for expectations and conventions.
Risk: fixation.

### Adjacent
Different product, similar workflow.
Examples:
property-management exception handling ↔ incident management;
approval workflow ↔ code review;
inventory reconciliation ↔ financial reconciliation.

Useful for interaction innovation.

### Distant
Different category, similar cognitive problem.
Examples:
airport departure board → high-density status scanning;
trading terminal → expert information throughput;
wayfinding → orientation and progressive decision making.

Use sparingly and transform the principle.

## Usability task design

A good test task:
- gives a realistic situation;
- specifies goal, not click path;
- has observable completion;
- avoids UI terminology that gives the answer away.

Observe:
- hesitation;
- wrong turns;
- recovery;
- missed status;
- terminology confusion;
- workarounds;
- confidence;
- information-seeking behavior.

## Insight structure

Use:
**Evidence → Interpretation → Design implication → Confidence**

Example:
Evidence: multiple operators opened history before approving.
Interpretation: approval depends on temporal context.
Implication: expose recent history before approval.
Confidence: medium.

Never fabricate:
- participant counts;
- quotes;
- success rates;
- NPS;
- research findings.
